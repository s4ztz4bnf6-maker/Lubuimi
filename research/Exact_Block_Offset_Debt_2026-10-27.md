# Exact block-offset identity and the fixed-start exception bound
Date: 2026-10-27
Status: algebraic derivation; not a global Collatz proof

## 1. Exact affine block identity

For the accelerated Syracuse map on positive odd integers,
\[
x_{i+1}=\frac{3x_i+1}{2^{a_i}},\qquad a_i=v_2(3x_i+1)\ge 1,
\]
let
\[
A_0=0,\qquad A_j=\sum_{i<j}a_i.
\]
Induction gives
\[
2^{A_L}x_L=3^Lx_0+C_L,\qquad
C_L=\sum_{j=0}^{L-1}3^{L-1-j}2^{A_j}.
\]
Normalize by \(3^L\) and define
\[
\lambda_L=\frac{2^{A_L}}{3^L},\qquad
q_L=\frac{C_L}{3^L}.
\]
Then the exact correction term has the useful prefix form
\[
\boxed{q_L=\sum_{j=0}^{L-1}\frac{2^{A_j}}{3^{j+1}}}
\]
and
\[
\boxed{x_L=\frac{x_0+q_L}{\lambda_L}}.
\]
Therefore descent is equivalent to
\[
q_L<(\lambda_L-1)x_0.
\]
This makes the proof obligation precise: control both the terminal multiplier \(\lambda_L\) and the accumulated prefix debt \(q_L\). A bound on the average of \(A_L\) alone is not yet a descent theorem.

## 2. Exact calculation for a block with every valuation equal to two

If \(a_i=2\) for \(0\le i<L\), then \(A_j=2j\), and
\[
\lambda_L=(4/3)^L,\qquad
q_L=\sum_{j=0}^{L-1}(4/3)^j/3=(4/3)^L-1.
\]
Consequently
\[
x_L=1+(x_0-1)(3/4)^L.
\]
There is also an exact arithmetic restriction for a *fixed* starting value. At each such step,
\[
x_{i+1}-1=\frac34(x_i-1),
\]
so after \(L\) steps
\[
x_L-1=\frac{3^L(x_0-1)}{4^L}.
\]
Since \(3^L\) is odd, integrality requires \(4^L\mid(x_0-1)\). Thus, if \(x_0>1\),
\[
\boxed{L\le\left\lfloor\frac{v_2(x_0-1)}2\right\rfloor.}
\]
The exceptional fixed point \(x_0=1\) has an infinite valuation-two orbit. This sharpens the statement “a fixed start cannot sustain an arbitrarily long valuation-two run”: the exact obstruction is the finite 2-adic valuation of \(x_0-1\).

## 3. Why this still does not solve Collatz

The bound depends on \(v_2(x_0-1)\), which is unbounded as the starting value ranges over odd integers. Hence it is not a uniform bound over all possible orbit states. The explicit long-run construction is consistent with this: its starting value is chosen with a correspondingly large power of two dividing \(x_0-1\).

More importantly, a general orbit can have mixed valuation words, not just a single run of twos. The unresolved global target is a theorem controlling the exact accumulated debt
\[
q_L=\sum_{j<L}2^{A_j}/3^{j+1}
\]
against \((\lambda_L-1)x_0\) for all admissible valuation words generated by the Syracuse congruences. No such uniform theorem is established here.

## 4. Falsifiable next target

A promising theorem shape is a *conditional debt bound*: assume a stated lower bound on every sufficiently long suffix sum
\[
A_L-A_j\quad (j<L),
\]
and derive a numerical upper bound on
\[
x_L= x_0\,3^L/2^{A_L}
 +\sum_{j<L}3^{L-1-j}2^{A_j-A_L}.
\]
The suffix sums matter because the offset contribution after division by \(2^{A_L}\) is
\[
\sum_{j<L}3^{L-1-j}2^{-(A_L-A_j)}.
\]
A correct theorem must state its suffix hypothesis explicitly and then prove that the hypothesis follows from the actual Syracuse orbit. The latter implication is currently the main open gap; it must not be replaced by a pseudorandomness or entropy assertion.

## 5. Scope and audit status

All identities above are exact algebra. This document is not a Lean kernel check, does not prove a uniform suffix bound, and does not prove or refute the Collatz conjecture.
